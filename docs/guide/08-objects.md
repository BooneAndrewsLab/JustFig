# Scientific objects

Scientific objects are things you would otherwise spend an hour drawing: a 96-well plate with coloured wells, a gel, a plasmid map, a lipid bilayer. You give JustFig a few settings and it draws the object. Because it stays an object, you can come back and change the settings at any time.

## Placing one

The **Scientific objects** shelf is at the top of the Icons panel. It can be folded away by clicking its title, and JustFig remembers whether it was open. Each tile shows a preview.

- **Click** a tile to place the object in the middle of what you can see.
- **Drag** a tile onto the canvas to drop it where you want it.
- **Search** for one in the icon search box: typing "plate", "gel", "agarose", "cell" or "umap" lists the matching scientific objects above the icons, each tagged **Adjustable**. Click, drag or press Enter on one exactly as on the shelf.

A scientific object is not the same as an icon of the same thing. An icon is a fixed drawing: you can recolour, resize and rotate it, but its shape never changes. A scientific object is redrawn from its settings, such as the number of wells, the lanes and bands, which organelles are shown or the plasmid's features, and resizing redraws it instead of stretching it.

Select an object to see its settings in the Inspector. Resize it with its handles; the object is redrawn at the new size. You can also rotate, flip and fade it like anything else.

## Starting points

Some objects come with ready-made **starting points**: a plant cell or a neuron, a bacterium, a Petri dish already streaked, a gel already loaded for a colony PCR, a well plate already laid out, a plasmid map of a common backbone, a timeline already set up for a time course. Under the shelf's tiles, open **Petri dish starting points** (or the gel's, plasmid map's or well plate's) to see them, each drawn as it will look. Click one to place it, or drag it onto the canvas. Searching for a starting point by name, such as "YPD", "blood agar" or "checkerboard", finds it too, tagged **Adjustable**.

On an object already in the figure, **Start from…** at the top of its Inspector section offers the same list. Choosing one changes only what that starting point is about and keeps the rest, in one step you can undo: a Patterns choice on a Petri dish keeps its medium, and a Media choice keeps its colonies. A starting point is only a start: every setting stays yours to change, and the figure does not remember which one you began from.

- **Cell** — Cell types: Animal cell, Plant cell (wall, central vacuole, chloroplasts), Budding yeast (wall, vacuole and a bud), Neuron, Polarised epithelial cell (microvilli on top), Mitosis (metaphase), Dividing cell (telophase) and Apoptotic cell. Each one sets the whole cell type, so choosing another replaces it.
- **Bacterium** — Bacteria: Gram-negative rod (E. coli) with flagella all round and pili, Gram-positive rod (Bacillus), Gram-positive coccus (Staphylococcus), Curved rod (Vibrio) with one flagellum, and Encapsulated rod (Klebsiella).
- **Petri dish** — Patterns: Single-colony streak, Spot dilutions, Strains by 10-fold dilutions, Pinned grid, Lawn, Disk diffusion (antibiotic discs), Phage plaques, Blue/white screening, Red/white sectored colonies, Wrinkled colonies, Tetrad dissection, Empty plate. Media (the agar and colony colours): LB, YPD, SC/dropout, Blood agar, MacConkey, X-gal (blue colonies), Chocolate agar.
- **Agarose gel** — Colony PCR check (a 1 kb ladder, six colonies screened for a 1.5 kb insert, with + and – controls) and Restriction digest (uncut plasmid, two single digests and the double digest).
- **Plasmid map** — Backbones: pUC19 (2,686 bp; AmpR, ori, lacZα, lac promoter, MCS, AmpR promoter) and pBR322 (4,361 bp; TetR, rop, ori, AmpR). Their positions were checked against the public GenBank records L09137 and J01749. Other vectors, such as pET-28a, are not offered because no public record of the empty vector could be checked; build them with Features, or copy them from PlasmidPop.
- **Well plate** — 96-well layouts: Controls in fixed columns (negative in column 1, positive in column 12), Blank edge wells (the outer ring left blank against edge effects), Dilution gradient (across the columns), Standard curve and triplicates (standards down columns 1 and 2, samples in triplicate, blanks in column 12) and Drug-combination checkerboard (one drug rising down the rows, the other across the columns).
- **Experiment timeline** — Time course (a stimulus, sampling ticks at 1, 4, 12 and 24 hours, harvest at 48) and Pooled CRISPR screen (transduction, puromycin selection, expansion, the screen and harvest over 28 days).
- **Study design** — Mouse treatment schedule (vehicle, drug and drug plus anti-PD-1 arms, implant, sampling and endpoint, drug bars over days 7 to 21) and Dosing with timed sampling (two arms dosed at hour 0 and sampled across 24 hours).

## Wizards

Some objects can be set up by answering a few questions instead. The **Plate map wizard…** button sits under **Well plate starting points** on the shelf (it adds a new plate) and beside **Start from…** in a well plate's Inspector section (it lays out that plate). It asks, one step at a time:

1. **Which plate?** The number of wells, 6 to 1536.
2. **What goes on it?** How many **Conditions** (samples, treatments or strains; each gets a colour of its own), how many **Replicates** of each, and whether the replicates go side by side in a row or one under another in a column.
3. **Where do the controls go?** **Controls**: none, negative only, or negative and positive. **Controls fill** the first and last columns or the first and last rows of the plate (negative grey, positive orange). And whether to leave the edge wells blank against edge effects; the controls then go just inside them.

A preview under the questions shows the plate as it will be, and a note says when not every condition fits (or when colours start to repeat after 24 conditions). **Next** (or Enter) goes on, **Back** goes back, and **Add** or **Apply** on the last step finishes; Escape or **Cancel** leaves without changing anything. Applying it to a plate already in the figure is one step you can undo and changes only its number of wells and its well colours. The answers are not remembered: the wizard starts from the same questions every time, and the plate is then yours to edit in the well-map editor.

### Plasmid map

Set the **Name**, the **Length** in bp and the **Topology** (Circular or Linear). Under **Features**, each feature has a name, **Start** and **End** (bp, counted from 1), **Strand** (Forward →, Reverse ← or None), **Type** and **Colour** (automatic unless you choose one). Click a feature to open it; use the arrow buttons to reorder, ✕ to remove and **Add** for a new one. You can show or hide **Name in the middle**, **Length**, **Scale ticks** and **Feature labels**, and set **Feature width** and **Label size**. Features shorter than **Smallest labelled feature** (1% of the length by default) are still drawn but have no label, which keeps dense maps such as pBR322 readable; set it to 0 to label every feature.

To bring in a real plasmid, copy it from PlasmidPop (see [Importing and other apps](11-import-and-other-apps.md)).

### Protein domains

Set the protein **Length** in aa. Each domain in the **Domains** list has a Name, Start, End, Colour and Shape (Rounded box, Box, Oval or Hexagon). Also available: **Domain height**, **Domain names**, **Residue ruler** and **N and C termini**.

### Petri dish

**Colonies** chooses the pattern: Scattered, Streak (three zones), Spot dilutions, Pinned grid, Lawn, Tetrad dissection or None (empty plate). **Number** sets how many colonies (not for Lawn or None), **Colony size** and **Size variation** the look, and **Lid rim** adds the dish rim. **Arrangement** works as on the cell.

Spot dilutions draws strains as rows and 10-fold dilutions as columns: the first spot is confluent and each next one has a tenth of the colonies. Set the number of **Strains (rows)** and **Dilutions (columns)**, and switch on **Row labels** to write A, B, C... beside each strain. **Number** is the colony count of the first countable spot. The _Strains by 10-fold dilutions_ starting point sets four rows, six columns and the labels.

On a Lawn, set the number of **Antibiotic discs** (each with a clear inhibition halo, whose **Halo width** you set) and of **Plaques** (clear spots where phage lysed the lawn, with their **Plaque size**). For plates of separate colonies, **Marker colour share** draws that percentage of them in the **Marker colonies** colour (blue/white screening, red/white _ade2_), and **Colony shape** makes them Smooth, Wrinkled, Filamentous, Petite (small) or Sectored (a wedge in the marker colour).

Tetrad dissection draws one column of four spores (a to d) per tetrad. Set the number of **Tetrads**, the **Segregation** (2:2, 4:0, 3:1, 1:3, 0:4, or mostly 2:2 with a few others), the share of **Dead spores** (a tiny microcolony) and **Missing spores** (an empty spot), and whether to number the tetrads. Spores carrying the marker are drawn in the **Marker colonies** colour, for example red _ade2_ against white.

### Agarose gel

Choose the **Ladder**: 1 kb ladder, 100 bp ladder or None. Under **Lanes**, give each lane a **Label**, tick **Ladder lane** to make it a ladder, and type the fragment sizes in **Bands (bp)**, separated by commas. Add an intensity after a colon to make a band fainter: `3000, 1200:0.4, 700` is three bands, the middle one at 40% strength. Bands are placed on a log scale as on a real gel. **Look** is Stained (light bands on dark) or Inverted (dark bands on light); you can also show **Lane labels**, **Ladder sizes** and **Wells**.

### Gel lane labels

For a photo of a real gel or blot. Select the picture and click **Label gel lanes** in its **Image** section: lane labels and ladder sizes are put over the picture and grouped with it, so they move, resize and rotate with it, and the labels are selected for you to fill in (later, open the group in the Layers panel to select them again). You can also place **Gel lane labels** from the shelf and size its box to the lanes yourself.

Under **Lanes**, give each lane a **Label** (an empty label leaves that lane unlabelled); add or remove rows to match the number of lanes on the photo. The lanes are evenly spaced from **First lane at** to **Last lane at**, the centres of the first and last lane as a percentage of the picture's width: adjust the two until every label sits over its lane. If the comb had a gap, give the lanes after it a **Shift** (a percentage of the width, plus or minus) to move each one off its even place. **Label angle** is Level, Slanted (45°) or Upright (90°) for long names. Each of the **Ladder sizes** has a **Label**, such as "3 kb", and a **Height**, how far down the picture its band is, as a percentage; **Sizes on** puts them on the Left or Right, with **Size ticks** pointing at the bands. **Text size** is in points and stays the same when you resize the picture, while the labels stay on their lanes.

### Scale bar

For a micrograph. Select the picture and click **Add scale bar** in its **Image** section: a scale bar is put in a corner of the picture and grouped with it, and **Image width** is filled in with the picture's width in pixels. **Pixel size** is filled in too when the picture came from a TIFF that states it (see Import and other apps); otherwise type it in µm per pixel, from the microscope software or the image's metadata (for example 0.065 µm/px). Then give the bar's **Bar length** in µm. The bar is then drawn at the right length for the picture, and stays right when you resize the group: only its length follows the picture, while **Thickness**, **Margin** and **Text size** stay in points. If you resample the picture (change its size in pixels) in another program before importing it, its pixel size changes too; cropping it does not. Cropping the picture with **Crop and rotate** (in the Inspector) keeps the bar calibrated: the bar's **Image width** is changed to the width of the crop. A magnified inset (**Make inset…**, see [The Inspector](06-inspector.md)) takes the picture's pixel size for its own scale bar.

The **Label** is written from the length (`10 µm`, `500 nm`, `1.5 mm`) unless you type your own **Label text**; untick **Label** to hide it, for example when the length is given in the legend. **Corner** puts the bar in any corner of the picture. The bar and text are white by default, for dark fluorescence images; change them in **Colours**. A bar longer than the picture is cut to the picture's width, so check the length if the bar runs edge to edge. You can also place a **Scale bar** from the shelf and size its box to a picture yourself.

### Experiment timeline

The "day 0 injection, day 7 boost, day 14 harvest" drawing. Set **Axis start**, **Axis end** and the **Unit** (Hours, Days or Weeks). **Tick labels** writes each tick as `Day 7` (or `24 h`), as plain numbers with the unit after the arrow, or not at all; **Tick step** is how far apart the labelled ticks are, and 0 picks one for you (a week of days, a day of hours, or a step that leaves the labels room). Smaller ticks mark every whole day, hour or week when there is room.

Under **Events**, each event has a **Label**, a **Time**, a **Marker** (Triangle, Tick or Bracket) and a **Colour** (the Markers colour unless you choose one). A bracket spans from its time to its **Bracket end**, for something that lasts a while, such as imaging on days 4 to 6. Labels that would run into each other are moved up a row, with a thin line down to their marker. **Event labels** puts them Above or Below the axis.

Under **Periods**, each period has a **Label**, **From**, **To** and **Colour**, and is drawn as a bar on the other side of the axis, below the tick labels: a drug in the water, a diet, serum starvation. Overlapping periods get a row each. A label that fits is written inside its bar, otherwise beside it. Events and periods outside the axis are not drawn.

**Arrow at the end** draws the axis as an arrow, and **Text size** is in points: resizing the timeline stretches the axis but keeps the text the same size.

### Sequence snippet

A short DNA, RNA or protein sequence set in the monospace font, one letter per column, for a figure that would otherwise be a hand-aligned text box. Paste the **Sequence**: letters, `-` (gap) and `*` (stop) are kept, lower case becomes upper case, and spaces, line breaks and numbers are dropped, so a sequence copied from a database entry works as it is. Only the first 200 residues are kept; a snippet is not a chromosome. The sequence wraps into rows of the object's width, so resizing the box changes how many residues fit in a row, while **Text size** (in points) does not change.

**First residue number** is the number of the first letter (for example 100 for a snippet starting at position 100). **Numbering ruler** writes a number above every residue whose number is a multiple of **Number every** (10 by default), and above the first residue of each wrapped row when there is room for it.

Under **Highlights**, each row has **From**, **To** and a **Colour**: the residues in that range, counted with the first residue number, get a coloured block behind them. Ranges outside the sequence are clipped. Under **Annotations**, each row has a **Label**, **From**, **To** and a **Colour** (the Annotations colour unless you choose one): a bracket is drawn over the residues, with the label above it. Give the same number in **From** and **To** to mark one residue, for example a mutation `p.R175H`, or a range for a primer or a tag. Labels that would run into each other are stacked in levels; an annotation that wraps onto the next row continues as a bracket without its label. The complementary strand is not drawn.

### Study design

For an animal or clinical study: several arms stacked on one time axis. Set **Axis start**, **Axis end**, **Unit** and **Tick labels** as for the experiment timeline.

Under **Arms** (1 to 8), give each arm a **Group** name and its **n**; they are written at the left as `Vehicle (n = 8)` (n of 0 leaves the n out), and **Guide line** is the colour of the thin line along the arm. Under **Markers**, each row puts one marker on an arm: **Arm** is the arm's number in the list (1 is the first), **Time** is where on the axis, and **Marker** is an Injection (triangle), Dosing (filled circle), Sampling (open circle) or Endpoint (cross). The four kinds have a colour each in **Colours**. Under **Treatment bars**, each bar has a **Label**, an **Arm** (0 draws it on every arm), **From**, **To** and a **Colour**, and sits under the markers of its arm; bars that overlap get a row each. **Legend of markers** adds a row naming the glyphs in use, **Arrow at the end** draws the axis as an arrow, and **Text size** is in points. Markers outside the axis or on an arm that does not exist are not drawn.

### Tree from Newick

A phylogenetic or clustering tree drawn from text, for a figure that would otherwise be a pasted bitmap. Paste a Newick string into **Newick** (up to 4,000 characters), for example `((Human:0.1,Chimp:0.12)99:0.06,Mouse:0.3);`. Branch lengths follow a colon, a label after a closing parenthesis is the clade's name or support value, quoted labels `'like this'` may hold spaces and punctuation, underscores in bare labels read as spaces, `[comments]` are skipped, and the final `;` is optional. Only the first tree of a string is drawn.

Choose a **Rectangular** layout (leaves in a column on the right, labels beside the tips) or a **Circular** one (leaves around a circle, labels turned along the radius and flipped upright on the left half). **Use branch lengths** draws the tree to scale; with it off, or when the string has no lengths, you get a cladogram with every tip at the same depth. **Show node labels** writes the values or names on the internal nodes, **Show leaf labels** the leaf names, and **Scale bar** adds a branch-length bar under a rectangular tree drawn to scale. **Text size** and **Line width** are in points: a tree with many leaves needs a taller object, since the labels do not shrink.

**Collapsed clades** draws a clade as a filled triangle with its name and leaf count, for example `Primates (12)`. Each row names the clade one of three ways: its label in the string (`Primates`), a leaf inside it (the clade directly above that leaf), or two leaves separated by a comma (`Human, Mouse`: the smallest clade holding both). A row that matches nothing is reported under the fields.

If the string cannot be read (an unbalanced parenthesis, a branch length that is not a number), the object shows a short red message in its frame and the Inspector says what is wrong and where, counted in characters.

### Placeholder plot

A plot-shaped drawing for when the figure needs "a UMAP goes here" rather than real data. The points are invented, so never use one where a reader could take it for a result.

**Plot type** is PCA (tilted clusters with axes labelled PC1 and PC2 and their share of variance), UMAP (lumpy clusters with two short corner arrows), Scatter (a rising cloud per group) or Bar chart (one bar per group). Under **Groups**, give each group a **Name** and a **Colour**; there can be 1 to 8, and each is a cluster, a cloud or a bar. **Points per group** sets how many points each cluster has (not for bar charts), **Spread** how loose the clusters are (or how long the error bars), and **Point size** their size.

**Flow cytometry** draws a cloud of made-up events (cells) on two log axes labelled 10^1 to 10^5, with the axis names CD4-FITC and CD8-PE (type your own in **x-axis label** and **y-axis label**). **Events** sets how many cells there are. **Gate** is None, Quadrant (two crossing lines with a percentage in each corner) or Polygon (a closed outline with one percentage). The percentages are not typed: they are the share of the drawn events inside each region, so they always match the dots. With a polygon gate, events inside it take the second group's colour and the rest the first group's; Points per group and the legend do not apply.

**Axes** and **Axis labels** switch those on and off. Leave **x-axis label** and **y-axis label** empty for the usual labels of the plot type, or type your own, such as "Dose (µM)". **Group names** shows a legend beside the plot, or the names under the bars of a bar chart. A bar chart can also show **Error bars** and **Data points** (six dots per bar).

**Arrangement** works as on the cell: the same number always draws the same points, so the plot never changes by itself. Use **Convert to shapes** to move single points by hand.

## The well-map editor

A Well plate has a **Well colours** editor in the Inspector: a small picture of the plate that you paint on.

1. Choose a **Brush** colour with the colour box or one of the quick colours.
2. **Click or drag** over wells to paint them. Hold **Alt** while you drag to erase, or tick **Eraser**.
3. **Clear** removes all well colours.

A whole stroke is one undo step. The note under the grid says how many wells are coloured.

**From the keyboard:** click the grid or Tab to it, then use the arrow keys to move the cursor (Home and End jump to the first and last column), **Space** or **Enter** to paint the well, **Delete** to clear it, and **Shift+arrows** to paint as you move. The grid announces each well, such as "B3: empty".

**From a spreadsheet:** choose **Paste from spreadsheet…**, copy a block of cells in Excel or Sheets (the top-left cell is well A1) and paste it into the box, then choose **Apply**.

**From a file:** choose **Open CSV/TSV file…** (or drop a `.csv`, `.tsv` or `.txt` file onto the well editor). The file's cells appear in the same box so you can check them before choosing **Apply**. Files must be plain text under 1 MB; quoted fields (`"a, b"`) and Excel's UTF-8 CSV exports work.

- Numbers become a light-to-dark colour ramp.
- Words become one colour each, so every "Control" well is the same colour.
- Colours written as `#d55e00` are used as they are.

A legend lists what the colours mean. Pasted wells are added to what is already painted.

## Turning a drawn path into a membrane, helix or proteins

Bilayers, DNA / RNA and membrane proteins follow a path. To use your own curve:

1. Draw a line or a pen path.
2. Select it. The Inspector shows a **Follow this path** section.
3. Choose **Lipid bilayer**, **DNA / RNA** or **Membrane proteins**.

The path is replaced by the object, which follows the same curve. Drag the object's handles to resize it. Undo brings your path back. To start without a path, place the object from the shelf; it comes with a default arch.

## Convert to shapes

**Convert to shapes**, at the foot of an object's settings, replaces the object with a group of ordinary shapes and text that you can edit one by one. Do this when you want to change something the settings cannot, such as moving a single colony. It is one-way apart from undo: the object stops being editable as a whole.

Converting does not change the figure's credit line, because these objects use no library icons.
